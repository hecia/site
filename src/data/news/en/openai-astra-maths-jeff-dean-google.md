OpenAI announced that an internal version of its upcoming model, Astra, had solved or made major progress on ten long-standing open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science. Among them, an explicit construction of a non-sofic group, an open question since 1999, and three problems from the Erdős catalog. Total compute cost claimed by OpenAI: about $2,000, a tiny amount relative to the result. The formal proofs (a 249-page manuscript, Lean 4 certificates) were published on GitHub under an Apache 2.0 license, fully machine-verified, which lets any mathematician check every step of the reasoning without having to take OpenAI's word for it.

[OpenAI's Astra solves 10 long-open math problems and publishes the proofs — SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/02/openais-astra-solves-10-long-open-math-problems-publishes-proofs/)

Jeff Dean is leaving Google to co-found Discovery Loop with Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le and Oriol Vinyals, a mission-driven company aiming to use AI to accelerate scientific research. Dean and Ghemawat had co-created MapReduce, Bigtable, Spanner and the Google File System, the architecture that enabled internet-scale computing and inspired much of the 2000s-2010s big data infrastructure (Hadoop being a direct descendant). Google remains a founding investor and cloud partner of the new company, which limits the scope of this departure: it's not a break, more a bet on a smaller, more focused structure.

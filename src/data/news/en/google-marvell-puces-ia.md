Marvell granted Google a right to purchase up to $12.2 billion of its own shares, in exchange for a chip purchase commitment (inference accelerators, memory and network controllers) that could generate about $120 billion in revenue for Marvell by 2033. The underlying commercial deal had been signed on July 29, with the financial announcement following three weeks later.

On the news, Marvell's stock jumped nearly 8%, while Broadcom, until then Google's main chip partner, fell more than 5%. A market move that mostly signals one thing: Google is diversifying its suppliers, and Broadcom is losing some of its near-monopoly on Google's custom chips.
